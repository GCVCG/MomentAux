# Referee report: Information Fusion

**Title.** When does fusing hand-crafted knowledge with learned representations pay? A controlled, cost-normalized benchmark and a measurable rule for stacking, substitution and interference

**Round.** Not stated in the submission context supplied. Treated as a fresh reading. No previous reviews and no response letter were provided.

**Declared contribution type.** Benchmark, with an accompanying method and an empirical decomposition.

**Central claim, reconstructed in one sentence.** Two information sources add value only when they supply different currencies, each source's currency is measurable in advance by a linear evaluation of its frozen features, and the visible gain from any source decomposes into a feature term plus a readout term governed by baseline accuracy.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper benchmarks a fixed, hand-crafted knowledge source, a pinned bank of Gabor quadrature pairs regressed as a training-only auxiliary target with a weight that decays to zero, against data-driven alternatives (SimCLR, SimSiam, DINO, ImageNet transfer, DeiT-strength augmentation, FitNets teachers, HOG targets) under one frozen recipe with fixed subset indices. The grid covers 13 datasets, 9 backbones, 500 to 1.28M images and 32 to 224 px, reported as 2,978 configurations over 9,079 runs, with segmentation and detection transplants added. Every pairwise combination is measured, and the outcomes are organised into three cases the authors call stack, substitute and tax, decided by whether the two sources supply the same currency. Underlying the grid is a decomposition of the end-to-end gain into a frozen-feature gain and a readout residual, whose sign is reported to hold on 85.7 percent of resolvable cells and which the authors used to make several pre-registered predictions. Two sensor experiments and a decision-level fusion experiment test whether the same rule reaches beyond training-time fusion.

## 2. Scope fit

This is the editor's call and I want to state the case on both sides rather than pretend it is settled. Applying the scope test: remove the word fusion and what remains is a data-efficiency benchmark for image classification, plus a training-only auxiliary loss, plus an empirical decomposition of where a gain shows up. That object belongs at a vision or learning venue. What pulls it back is Section 2.4, which is a serious and accurate engagement with the fusion literature (Durrant-Whyte, Khaleghi, Dasarathy, Smirnov, partial information decomposition, catastrophic fusion, modality competition), and the von Rüden reading under which data and prior knowledge are two sources. That reading is defensible, but it is broad enough that any auxiliary loss or pretrained initialization becomes a fusion study, and the editor should decide whether the journal wants that boundary. The parts that are fusion in the journal's mainstream sense are the thinnest in the paper: Section 6.1 is one backbone, three seeds, two populations, and its headline statistic was withdrawn during preparation; Section 6.2 rests on 28 pairs from a single cell, narrowed to three matched pairs. My own view is that the paper sits inside the journal's stated interest in knowledge-and-data fusion, but the multi-source evidence should be strengthened or the fusion claims narrowed to what training-time fusion supports.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The comparator budget section (Section 7, Tables 14 and 15) overturns two of the authors' own accuracy claims and says so.** Raising SimCLR to five times its pre-training budget makes it beat the prior at every convolutional fraction by 3.10 to 9.23 points, and the paper restates its own contribution as a cost claim rather than an accuracy claim. Comparators are almost never bought more compute in this literature, and never when it costs the authors a result.

2. **The audit scope repair (Section 4.2) is reported with the excluded cells' own rate.** The headline moved from 79.1 to 85.7 percent after a string-matching bug in a filter was fixed. The paper gives the previous headline, the 50 resolvable cells that left, and their own agreement rate of 18.0 percent. Reporting the second of those is what makes the first checkable.

3. **Reproducibility is genuinely built in, not asserted.** A per-cell results table, released subset indices, numerically fingerprinted filter banks, per-run records with checksums, and the sign-law audit shipped as a script rather than a query. The prose numbers are generated from the run records by macro, so a figure in the text cannot drift from the table beside it. I recomputed every derived quantity I could reach from the tables (audit partitions, Wilson intervals, the exact binomial, the compute and carbon chain, all margin subtractions in Tables 14 and 15) and found one discrepancy, listed at item N5 below.

4. **Mechanism controls that could have failed and did not.** A random fixed target of identical shape moves accuracy by at most 0.12 points, a FitNets teacher at twice the cost does nothing across nine fractions, and the imprint experiment (Table 10) produces a two-by-two in which alignment follows the target while feature gain follows the initialization, against a registered band that the self-supervised arms broke in the opposite direction.

5. **Negatives carried as results.** The detection null (Section 9.7), the 50-epoch dense grid that produced the opposite scientific answer twice (Section 9.3), and the withdrawn asymmetry correlation (Section 6.1) are all in the main text.

## 4. Blocking issues

**B1. The readout term's negative branch may be an artifact of the probe's label budget.** Location: Section 3.4 and Eq. 5, Section 4.1, Tables 7 and 8. The linear evaluation is fit on the full training split while the cell trained on p percent of it, and Section 3.4 states explicitly that the evaluation is interpretable only while it holds substantially more labels than the cell. At 1 percent that is a hundredfold label advantage. A probe with far more labels will show more of a feature improvement than the few-label end-to-end classifier realizes, so a negative residual follows from the protocol before any learning phenomenon is invoked. Baseline accuracy is a monotone proxy for that same label ratio, so the statement that the readout is negative below a baseline-accuracy crossing partly restates the asymmetry. This blocks because it is where the evidence is: 311 of 461 resolvable cells sit below the crossing, and 293 of the 395 correct calls come from that flank. **Necessary.** Re-fit the probe at the cell's own label budget on a subset of cells spanning the crossing, and report whether the negative branch survives. The paper states that probes run in minutes on cells that took hours to train, so this is well inside a revision window.

**B2. The audit is scored against a null that no one would propose.** Location: Section 4.2, Tables 7 and 8. The one-sided exact binomial against an even coin gives p near 2 by 10 to the minus 58, which I reproduce, but a coin is not a competitor to the account. Table 8's own split implies that 341 of the 461 resolvable cells carry a negative readout, so a constant rule that always predicts negative scores 74.0 percent against the law's 85.7 percent. The account still wins, and the Wilson interval of 82.2 to 88.6 clears 74.0, but the effect size a reader takes away from a p-value of that magnitude is not the effect size in the data. **Necessary.** Report the majority-sign baseline, and add a permutation test that shuffles baseline accuracy across cells within a dataset, so the reported margin is against a null that holds the marginal sign distribution fixed.

**B3. The headline ViT-B result may be the stabilization effect, which the paper elsewhere treats as separate.** Location: Table 12 and Section 5; compare Section 3.5 and Section 8.6. Section 3.5 declares that any cell with a seed collapsing to chance is flagged bistable and excluded from headline status. Section 8.6 reports stabilization as a real property distinct from accuracy gain. Section 5 then records that the ViT-B baseline's seed variance is three times the prior arm's, and reports a gain of 26.01 on a baseline of 43.3, against ViT-S's 65.4. This is the paper's most promoted number: abstract, highlights, contributions, discussion and conclusion. If part of it is the baseline arm failing to train on some seeds, then the model-scale trend that carries the conclusion is confounded with an effect the paper itself keeps separate everywhere else. **Necessary.** Print per-seed accuracies for both arms of the ViT-S and ViT-B cells, print the SEM of each delta in Table 12, state whether the ViT-B cell passes the Section 3.5 rule, and if the gain is partly stabilization, say so where the number is quoted.

**B4. The envelope shape is confounded with the optimization step budget.** Location: Section 3.1, Tables 6, 9 and 17. The recipe fixes epochs rather than steps, so a 1 percent cell trains for a small fraction of the steps a 100 percent cell does, and the auxiliary weight's cosine decay is spread over that smaller number of steps. The unimodal envelope across the data axis is a headline claim of the paper and the object the law is built to explain. Section 7.3 shows the authors already know this matters: they diagnose the comparator at 5 percent as step-starved rather than data-starved, on 200 epochs over 2,500 images being fewer than four thousand steps. The same argument applies to the prior's own envelope and is not addressed there. **Necessary.** On one dataset and one backbone, measure the envelope at a fixed total step budget across fractions, or hold data fixed and vary epochs, and show the shape is a data effect rather than a step effect.

**B5. The pre-registration is the paper's central credibility argument and a reviewer cannot check it.** Location: Section 3.8, and the registered bands in Sections 4.3, 5, 6.1, 9.4 and 9.7. The paper's defence against the charge that the law is a curve fit is that bands and falsifiers were recorded in a project ledger before each wave of runs. That ledger is not in the released artifacts listed under Data availability or Appendix F. As it stands the registrations are unverifiable assertions in a paper that is otherwise scrupulous about releasing its evidence. **Necessary.** Release the ledger with commit hashes or timestamps, one entry per registered band, and cite it at each point where a registration is claimed.

**B6. Section 6.3's takeaway contradicts Section 6.1.** Location: final paragraph of Section 6.3 against the withdrawal in Section 6.1. Section 6.1 withdraws the correlation of minus 0.80, states that the two sensor populations differ in both asymmetry and modality, and concludes that the experiment supports a contrast and not a predictor. The takeaway then states that across sensors and across classifiers, how asymmetric two sources are predicts whether combining them pays. The abstract gets this right; the takeaway does not. **Necessary**, and it is editing rather than experiment. Restate the takeaway to the sensor evidence as Section 6.1 leaves it, and attribute the asymmetry claim to the 28-pair decision-level fit alone.

## 5. Non-blocking issues

**N1. The target-family ablation is selection-contaminated and carries a mechanism claim.** Table 18, with Section 3.6. Section 3.6 discloses that CIFAR-100 is the selection set scored on the same test split, and defends the general claims on the twelve transplant populations. That defence does not cover Table 18, because only the champion configuration transplanted. Table 18 is the sole evidence for "the target is the ingredient", which Sections 2.2 and 10.1 both rely on. Repeating six target rows at two fractions on one transplant dataset is small against the grid already run. Necessary if the mechanism claim is to be kept in its current strength; optional if it is softened.

**N2. The transfer tax and the word "never".** Table 11 shows three cells, and Table 19 turns them into "never add prior" for any ImageNet-transferable task. The audit scope repair implies many more transfer cells exist. Report the full transfer-tax population rather than three illustrative rows. Separately, the tax is measured only with the auxiliary weight at full strength from step zero; a delayed onset or reduced weight on a pretrained initialization is untested, so "never" is stronger than the design supports. The delayed-onset run is optional.

**N3. Algorithm 1 is validated prospectively once.** Section 6.3 runs the procedure end to end on one pair and contrasts it with one other. Contribution 5 is stated as a procedure. Two further prospective runs, one expected to return stack and one tax, would move this from illustration to evidence. Optional but strongly advised, since this is a headline contribution.

**N4. Three attributions to prior work carry no citation.** The cookbook-level guidance for large-scale self-supervised use (Section 2, self-supervised paragraph); "reality-check studies have repeatedly shown that uncontrolled recipes manufacture illusory progress" together with the reproducibility claim (end of Section 2.6); and the decomposition of learning dynamics into representation and readout components proposed for grokking and double descent (end of Section 2.5). The last is the closest antecedent to the paper's own contribution and needs naming. The reference list stands at exactly 50, which is the journal's cap, so adding these means dropping others.

**N5. Compute accounting, and one arithmetic slip.**
- Section 3.11 states that two to three trainings are run per GPU, then reports GPU-hours by aggregating the wall-clock recorded in each run record. Summing per-run wall-clock across runs that shared a device overstates device time by the concurrency factor. The 3,518 GPU-hours figure, and therefore the 2,787 kWh and 474 kg figures derived from it, may be two to three times high. State whether the aggregation divides by concurrency; if it does not, either correct it or say plainly that the figure is run-hours and an upper bound.
- Table 24 reports the SimCLR minus prior margin at 1 percent as plus 0.85. Table 14's own entries give 2.25 minus 1.42, that is 0.83, and no rounding of the displayed values reaches 0.85. Every other fraction in Table 24 reconciles exactly with Table 14.

**N6. Table 12 prints no uncertainty.** Table 6 prints the seed-paired standard error on every entry, with a caption arguing that these errors are what decide which comparisons are readable. Table 12 carries the paper's largest claims and prints none, except in a footnote. Add the SEM column.

**N7. The Swin row in Table 21 appears to be a diagnostic cell in a headline table.** Section 9.5 states that the dense Swin cells are AdamW diagnostics. Section 3.1 states that diagnostic cells never enter a headline table. Either mark the row or explain why Table 21 is not headline.

**N8. Two claims cite evidence that is not in the paper.** Section 5.1 gives the Tiny-ImageNet amplification ratio as 1.3 to 2.4 times, but Table 9 has no DeiT column for Tiny-ImageNet, so the ratio cannot be checked. Section 2.2 says HOG recovers roughly half the moment target's gain, while Table 18 and its caption say a third to a half, and the 5 percent column gives a third.

## 6. Questions for the authors

1. What does the readout term look like when the linear evaluation is fit at the cell's own label budget rather than on the full training split? If the negative branch shrinks or disappears, how should the law be restated?
2. What are the per-seed accuracies of both arms of the ImageNet-100 ViT-B and ViT-S cells, and does the ViT-B baseline pass the bistability rule declared in Section 3.5?
3. Does the unimodal envelope survive at a fixed optimization step budget across data fractions?
4. Can the pre-registration ledger be released with timestamps, so the registered bands in Sections 4.3, 5 and 9 are checkable?
5. In Table 9, ViT-tiny under the plain recipe reads plus 1.4 and plus 3.3 at 1 and 2 percent on CIFAR-100, and plus 1.4 and plus 3.3 at the same fractions on Tiny-ImageNet, while all seven remaining rows differ. Are these two independent measurements?

## 7. Minor and editorial

Numbers and tables. The abstract is at exactly 250 words with macros expanded, which leaves no margin against the journal's limit; it also carries a URL that already appears in the title-page footnote. Table 4 states the data scale as 500 to 1.28M images, but Table 5 lists trained cells at 188, 200, 216, 243 and 282 images, so the lower bound is wrong wherever it is repeated (abstract, introduction, Section 11.1). Appendix E states that five relabeled controls are listed at the foot of Table 5; Table 5 has no such rows. Section 9.2 gives the relative dense band as 1 to 22 percent across 1 to 25 percent of the data, but the 1 comes from a 100 percent cell and the minimum inside that band is 4 percent. Section 9.6 says the label-space effect is at most about 1.6 times in relative terms; the 10 percent column of Table 21 gives 9 against 4, that is 2.25 times. Table 3's amplification ratio at 1 percent reads 2.4 where the displayed values give 2.29. In Table 7, the last column of Table 7 mixes values at 25 and 100 percent under one header with an asterisk for two rows only; give the fraction per cell.

Presentation. There are 24 numbered tables and 13 figures. Several tables carry five rows or fewer and would read better merged or as prose, which would also relieve the page budget. Every table is set at scriptsize. The alternating row shading runs against the journal's own table guidance, and production is likely to strip it, so the guide-literal setting is the safer choice. The title runs to 24 words. Section 4.4's opening sentence has a missing conjunction ("invariant to which coarse partition relabels the data but cut roughly from 4.2 to 2.9"). Section 4.2 has an unclosed clause at "on ViT it is exactly neutral against the better single their feature gains are the same gain". Section 4.6 has a stray space before a colon at "What this does not establish :". Section 7.4 says the band "missed low by 3.5" where the band sat above the outcome; rephrase.

English quality, reported separately: the prose is clear, controlled and better than typical for this venue. Nothing in the language stands in for a technical judgment above.

## 8. Confidential comments to the editor

No misconduct concern. There are zero self-citations across 50 references, which is unusual in the other direction, and the disclosures are more candid than the field's norm: a selection-set contamination admitted as a design defect, a filter bug that moved a headline number reported with the excluded cells' own rate, two accuracy claims withdrawn after buying the comparator more compute, and four failed pre-registered predictions reported as such. I recomputed every derived quantity reachable from the tables, including the audit partitions, all three Wilson intervals, the exact binomial, the compute-to-carbon chain and every margin subtraction in Tables 14, 15 and 24, and found exactly one that does not reconcile (item N5). That is a good sign about the underlying record-keeping.

On salvageability: yes, within one round. B3, B5 and B6 cost nothing but disclosure and editing. B1 and B2 are analysis reruns on existing checkpoints. B4 is the only new training, and it is one dataset and one backbone. None of the six requires a different paper.

This should not have been desk rejected on quality. The scope decision is genuinely yours and I would not overrule an editor who called it out of scope, because the fusion framing does most of its work in Section 2.4 and the genuinely multi-source experiments are the weakest evidence in the manuscript. If you keep it, I would ask the authors to either strengthen Section 6.1 with the third population they themselves identify as the missing control, or reduce the sensor claims to a reported contrast and move the fusion-theory positioning to the front of the paper rather than the middle.

Two practical notes. The archive's own SUBMISSION_FILES.md lists a response letter as part of the upload package, and the internal comments in main.tex refer to earlier review rounds. If this is a revision, I was not sent the previous reports or the response letter and could not weigh the revision on whether the earlier blocking issues are gone. Second, I received LaTeX source rather than a compiled PDF, and no access to the released repository or run records, so every check above is internal consistency only.

## 9. Recommendation and confidence

**Major revision.** The central idea is worth this journal if the scope question resolves in the paper's favour, the instrument is unusually well built, and the missing evidence for every blocking issue is obtainable in a normal revision window. What is not yet supported is the strength in which the law is stated: its evidence sits almost entirely on a flank where the measurement protocol supplies a mechanical reason for the predicted sign, and it is benchmarked against a null that inflates the apparent effect.

**Confidence: 4 of 5.** I could not verify the pre-registration ledger, the released artifacts, the compiled page count, or any number against the underlying run records, and I did not independently assess the segmentation and detection training setups in Section 9 at the level I assessed the classification grid. The first of those limits how much weight I can put on the prediction-not-fit argument, which is the paper's main defence of its central contribution.
