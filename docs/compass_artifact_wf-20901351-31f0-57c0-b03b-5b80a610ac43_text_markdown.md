# Situating MomentAux: A Literature Survey of Fixed Image-Moment Priors, Auxiliary Feature-Prediction, and Data-Scale-Aware Inductive Bias

## TL;DR
- MomentAux's core mechanism — a **fixed, hand-crafted image-moment bank (Gabor/Zernike/complex-Gabor energy) used as a jointly-trained auxiliary MSE regression target on an intermediate layer of a plain CNN classifier, deployed with zero inference overhead and empirically positive-or-neutral at every data scale** — is not covered by any existing paper we located; the nearest neighbors are Bhattarai et al. (2023, HOG-auxiliary joint task, but for segmentation), Mostajabi et al. (2018, discardable joint auxiliary regression head, but a *learned* target), and the FitNets/MaskFeat feature-prediction lineage (which all use *learned* teachers or a *pretraining* regime).
- The forward-path finding (fixed channels help only at ≤5% data and *hurt* in a 10–25% "penalty band") is strongly consistent with the broad, well-documented literature on structured/fixed front-ends (scattering, Gabor-CNN, PCANet): fixed features are data-efficient in the small-sample regime but cap or drag accuracy once enough data is available to learn better filters.
- The "harmless-at-scale" behavior of MomentAux is the empirical analogue of the classic Bayesian result that a prior dominates the posterior at small N and is washed out by the likelihood as N grows; MomentAux realizes this as a *soft* representational prior (a loss term) rather than a *hard* architectural constraint, which is exactly why it avoids the penalty band the forward-path variant suffers.

## Key Findings
1. **Fixed filter banks are a data-scale trade-off, not a free lunch.** Across scattering networks (Bruna & Mallat 2013; Oyallon et al. 2017), Gabor-CNNs (Luan et al. 2018; Alekseev & Bobe 2019), and PCANet (Chan et al. 2015), fixed/structured front-ends deliver strong small-sample and low-compute performance but are repeatedly shown to be matched or surpassed by fully-learned features once data is plentiful. Oyallon et al. explicitly report that hybrid architectures "yield excellent performance in the small sample regime, exceeding their end-to-end counterparts, through their ability to incorporate geometrical priors." This directly corroborates MomentAux's forward-path result.
2. **Equivariance/invariance priors show the same "bias helps until data overrides it" curve.** Group-equivariant and steerable CNNs (Cohen & Welling 2016; Weiler & Cesa 2019; Worrall et al. 2017) reduce sample complexity by hard-coding symmetries; the CNN-vs-ViT literature (Dosovitskiy et al. 2021) formalizes that strong inductive bias wins in low data and is overtaken at scale. The complex-cell energy model (phase invariance via squared quadrature filters) is the biological/mathematical basis for MomentAux's complex-Gabor energy channel.
3. **Auxiliary feature-prediction is the closest family — but the specific configuration is open.** Deep supervision (Lee et al. 2015), self-supervised auxiliary losses (Gidaris et al. 2018; Hendrycks et al. 2019), and feature distillation (Hinton et al. 2015; Romero et al. 2015; Zagoruyko & Komodakis 2017) all attach extra heads/losses to shape representations. But classical distillation targets are *learned* (a trained teacher's activations), and hand-crafted-feature prediction (MaskFeat, Wei et al. 2022; "Encoding Human Design," Liu et al. 2015) is done as *self-supervised pretraining* that deploys a fine-tuned backbone. Only Bhattarai et al. (2023) uses a fixed hand-crafted descriptor (HOG) as a *jointly-trained* auxiliary target — and that is for segmentation, not classification, and uses HOG rather than moments.
4. **The "washes out with data" property is principled.** Bayesian posterior-consistency (the prior is dominated by the likelihood as N→∞) is the theoretical frame for MomentAux's empirically observed scaling (+2.8 pts at 10% data, ~0 at 100%). Casting the moment prior as a soft loss term rather than a hard input constraint is what lets the data override it gracefully instead of paying a fixed architectural tax.

## Details

### 1. Structured / fixed filter banks in deep vision
The **scattering transform** (Bruna & Mallat, *Invariant Scattering Convolution Networks*, IEEE TPAMI 35(8):1872–1886, 2013; arXiv:1203.1513) cascades fixed wavelet convolutions with modulus nonlinearities and averaging to produce translation-invariant, deformation-stable representations; it set strong results "for handwritten digits and texture discrimination" and, notably, "over small training sets." **Oyallon, Belilovsky & Zagoruyko** (*Scaling the Scattering Transform: Deep Hybrid Networks*, ICCV 2017; arXiv:1703.08961) used the scattering network as a *fixed initialization* of a CNN's early layers, achieving a "single-crop top-5 error of 11.4% on ImageNet ILSVRC2012, comparable to the ResNet-18 architecture, while utilizing only 10 layers," and reported verbatim that "we find that hybrid architectures can yield excellent performance in the small sample regime, exceeding their end-to-end counterparts, through their ability to incorporate geometrical priors." This is the single most direct precedent for the *idea* that fixed geometric priors help most when data is scarce.

**Gabor-CNNs** come in two flavors. Luan et al. (*Gabor Convolutional Networks*, arXiv:1705.01450) modulate learnable conv kernels with fixed Gabor filters to inject orientation/scale robustness with fewer parameters. Alekseev & Bobe (*GaborNet*, arXiv:1904.13204) make Gabor parameters learnable in the first layer; tellingly, they report that a Gabor-constrained AlexNet's advantage over a plain AlexNet *shrinks to near-zero by the end of training* ("By the end of the training stage, Gabor Alexnet and Alexnet achieved almost exact results"), and a separate study documents learnable-Gabor "filter degeneration after a few epochs" (Springer, 10.1007/978-3-031-62836-8_36). Both observations echo MomentAux's forward-path phenomenology: the fixed prior is committed early and either becomes neutral or degrades.

**Zernike moments** are orthogonal, rotation-invariant shape descriptors (Khotanzad & Hong, IEEE TPAMI 1990). In deep learning they have been used as fixed convolution-kernel initializers (Mahesh, Joseph Raj & Fan, *Invariant moments based CNNs*, Int. J. Computational Intelligence Systems, 10.2991/ijcis.2017.10.1.62) and as pooling/descriptors fused into DCNNs (multi-channel Zernike moments, ScienceDirect S1566253523001392). **PCANet** (Chan et al., IEEE TIP 24(12):5017–5032, 2015; arXiv:1404.3606) showed a fixed PCA filter bank + hashing + histograms is "on par with the state of the art" on face/digit tasks — a canonical demonstration that fixed features are a strong *baseline* that learned deep nets eventually beat on richer tasks.

*Synthesis.* The literature is consistent and unambiguous: fixed front-ends (scattering, Gabor, PCANet, moment kernels) buy sample-efficiency and interpretability but tend to cap accuracy at scale, and their benefit is concentrated in the small-data regime. MomentAux's forward-path result (help ≤5%, hurt 10–25%, neutral at 100%) is the fine-grained, seed-averaged confirmation of this qualitative consensus — and its diagnosis (fixed channels compete for input bandwidth and get committed during the high-LR phase) is a novel, sharper account of *why* the mid-data penalty appears.

### 2. Equivariance & invariance as priors
**Group-equivariant CNNs** (Cohen & Welling, *Group Equivariant Convolutional Networks*, ICML 2016; arXiv:1602.07576) generalize convolution to symmetry groups, "reduc[ing] sample complexity by exploiting symmetries." **General E(2)-Equivariant Steerable CNNs** (Weiler & Cesa, NeurIPS 2019; arXiv:1911.08251) unify rotation/reflection-equivariant designs; importantly, they note "the final kernels can be stored such that during test time steerable CNNs are computationally not more expensive than conventional CNNs" — a parallel to MomentAux's zero-overhead goal, though achieved architecturally rather than via a loss. **Harmonic Networks** (Worrall et al., CVPR 2017; arXiv:1612.04642) replace filters with circular harmonics for patch-wise rotation equivariance.

The **energy model of complex cells** — responses as the sum of squares of two phase-shifted (quadrature) linear filters, yielding spatial-phase invariance (Adelson & Bergen 1985; see review at PMC7954310) — is the direct antecedent of MomentAux's complex-Gabor *energy* channel. This gives the phase-invariant moment a principled, biologically-grounded justification as an inductive prior.

The **inductive-bias-vs-data-scale** trade-off is now well quantified: CNNs' locality/translation-equivariance biases make them data-efficient, while ViTs (Dosovitskiy et al., *An Image is Worth 16x16 Words*, ICLR 2021) need large-scale pretraining to overtake them; hybrids that inject convolutional bias (e.g., convolutional-initialization for ViTs, arXiv:2401.12511) recover small-data performance. Empirically, several works show the crossover explicitly across data fractions (e.g., ImageNet subsets at 1%/5%/10%/50%, arXiv:2210.01370).

*Synthesis.* Hard-coded invariance is the architectural sibling of MomentAux's prior; the whole family exhibits "bias helps at small N, is overtaken at large N." MomentAux differs by making the invariance a *target to predict* rather than a *constraint to obey*, so the network can partially ignore it when data provides better evidence — a softer knob than equivariant architectures allow.

### 3. Auxiliary losses & feature-prediction training (the closest family)
**Deep supervision** (Lee et al., *Deeply-Supervised Nets*, AISTATS 2015) attaches auxiliary classifiers to intermediate layers to improve gradient flow; GoogLeNet's auxiliary heads (Szegedy et al. 2015) are the famous instance. **Self-supervised auxiliary losses** added *jointly* to supervised training improve robustness and low-data performance: Hendrycks, Mazeika, Kadavath & Song (*Using Self-Supervised Learning Can Improve Model Robustness and Uncertainty*, NeurIPS 2019; arXiv:1906.12340) show that auxiliary rotation prediction "maintains clean accuracy at 95.5% but increases the average accuracy on corrupted images by 4.6% to 76.9%" (vs. a 94.7% clean / 72.3% corrupted baseline, 40-2 Wide ResNets on CIFAR-10-C), and elsewhere that auxiliary rotation prediction with no loss correction can outperform doubling trusted data against label noise; Gidaris et al. (*Boosting Few-Shot Visual Learning with Self-Supervision*, ICCV 2019) add rotation/location prediction to few-shot learners. These establish the *joint auxiliary head that is discarded at inference* pattern — but their targets are transformations of the input, not fixed descriptors.

**Knowledge/feature distillation** is the mechanically closest family. Hinton, Vinyals & Dean (*Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network*, NIPS-W 2014; arXiv:1503.02531) match a student to a trained ensemble's soft outputs. FitNets (Romero, Ballas, Kahou, Chassang, Gatta & Bengio, ICLR 2015; arXiv:1412.6550) add *intermediate-representation* hints: "using not only the outputs but also the intermediate representations learned by the teacher as hints." Attention transfer (Zagoruyko & Komodakis, ICLR 2017; arXiv:1612.03928) matches spatial attention maps. **Crucially, all of these regress onto a *learned* teacher's activations** — a data-dependent signal requiring an extra trained model — whereas MomentAux regresses onto a *fixed, deterministic, hand-crafted* moment map computable directly from the input with no teacher.

**Hand-crafted-feature prediction** is where the gap narrows most. MaskFeat (Wei, Fan, Xie, Wu, Yuille & Feichtenhofer, *Masked Feature Prediction for Self-Supervised Visual Pre-Training*, CVPR 2022; arXiv:2112.09133) predicts HOG — verbatim, "We study five different types of features and find Histograms of Oriented Gradients (HOG), a hand-crafted feature descriptor, works particularly well in terms of both performance and efficiency" — but as a *masked self-supervised pretraining* task on Transformers (ViT/MViT) that deploys a *fine-tuned* backbone, with no concurrent cross-entropy; its own multi-task ablation (Table 13) found that combining HOG with pixel targets *hurt* accuracy. "Encoding Human Design" (Liu, Mallya, Tuzel & Chen, arXiv:1502.05689) pretrains CNNs to replicate HOG/region-covariance features via Euclidean regression, then fine-tunes — again sequential pretraining, not a joint target. The single closest published mechanism is **Bhattarai, Subedi, Gaire, Vazquez & Stoyanov** (*Histogram of Oriented Gradients meet deep learning: A novel multi-task deep network for 2D surgical image semantic segmentation*, Medical Image Analysis 85:102747, 2023; arXiv:2204.01712): they "exploited these [HOG] representations as labels of an auxiliary task" and, verbatim, "Together with the ground truth semantic segmentation masks for the primary task and pseudo-labels for the auxiliary task, we learn the parameters of the deep network to minimize the loss of both the primary task and the auxiliary task jointly." But their primary task is **semantic segmentation** (not classification), the descriptor is HOG (not moments), the backbones are UNet and U²Net, and the work targets surgical imaging (it won the FetReg Endovis sub-challenge at MICCAI 2021). Structurally, **Mostajabi, Maire & Shakhnarovich** (*Regularizing Deep Networks by Modeling and Predicting Label Structure*, CVPR 2018; arXiv:1804.02009) is the nearest analogue of the *discardable joint auxiliary regression head trained from scratch* — "after training, we discard this auxiliary branch" — but their target is a *learned* label-autoencoder, not a hand-crafted descriptor.

*Synthesis.* MomentAux sits at an unoccupied intersection: (fixed hand-crafted *moment* target) × (joint auxiliary regression alongside classification cross-entropy) × (plain-CNN classifier trained from scratch) × (zero-overhead deployment). Each individual ingredient exists in prior art, but no located work combines all four, and the specific descriptor family (Gabor/Zernike/complex-Gabor energy moments as a *prediction target*) appears novel.

### 4. Priors that "wash out" with data
Bayesian posterior consistency is the textbook statement of MomentAux's data-scaling behavior: "with a larger sample size, the likelihood dominates the posterior... with a small sample size, the prior has relatively more weight" (Frontiers in Psychology, 10.3389/fpsyg.2020.611963), and formally the posterior concentrates on the truth as N grows regardless of a (well-supported) prior. The CNN-vs-ViT inductive-bias literature is the deep-learning instantiation of this: strong priors help in low data and are overridden at scale. What is comparatively rare in the literature is a *method explicitly engineered to be harmless-at-scale* — a prior that provably cannot hurt as data grows. MomentAux's soft-loss formulation is what delivers this: because the prior enters as a weighted regression term (λ) rather than a hard architectural constraint, gradient descent can down-weight its influence when the classification signal is strong, so the empirical curve is positive-or-neutral everywhere. The forward-path variant lacks this escape hatch — the fixed channels are a permanent bandwidth tax — which is why it exhibits the mid-data penalty band.

### 5. Low-data / small-sample image classification & protocols
The **data-fraction sweep** (train on p% of the training set, evaluate on the full test set, repeat over seeds) is an established protocol; X-CNN (Koutník et al., arXiv:1610.00163) used exactly this on CIFAR-10/100 ("only p% of the entire training dataset"), and modern tiny-regime studies use the ResNet-18 + SGD + cosine + 200-epoch recipe with multiple seeds (e.g., arXiv:2603.07365 uses "SGD... cosine annealing... over 200 epochs... five random seeds") that MomentAux mirrors. This distinguishes the **low-data** regime (a fraction of a standard dataset, full classes) from **few-shot** learning (k examples per novel class with meta-learning). A caveat specific to CIFAR: Barz & Denzler (*Do We Train on Test Data? Purging CIFAR of Near-Duplicates*, Journal of Imaging 6(6):41, 2020; DOI 10.3390/jimaging6060041; arXiv:1902.00423) found "3.3% and 10% of the images from the test sets of these datasets have duplicates in the training set" — for CIFAR-100 specifically, 891 test images duplicated from training plus 104 within-test duplicates — and their corrected ciFAIR re-evaluation raised CIFAR-100 error by 2.73 percentage points on average, though the relative ranking of architectures was preserved.

*Synthesis.* MomentAux's evaluation protocol (ResNet-18, CIFAR-100, SGD/cosine/200 epochs, fractions 1–100%, 3 seeds) is squarely within accepted practice; the contribution is not the protocol but the fine-grained mapping of *where* a fixed prior helps vs. hurts, and a method that removes the harm.

### Novelty & gap statement
The combination that defines MomentAux is not present in the located literature:
1. **Fixed hand-crafted *moment* features (Gabor/Zernike/complex-Gabor energy) as an auxiliary *prediction target*.** Prior hand-crafted-target work uses HOG (MaskFeat, Encoding Human Design, Bhattarai et al.). Moments-in-deep-nets work uses them as *input channels, kernel initializers, or pooled descriptors* — never as a regression target for an intermediate layer.
2. **Joint training with classification cross-entropy, from scratch** — not pretraining (MaskFeat, Encoding Human Design) and not teacher distillation (Hinton, FitNets, attention transfer).
3. **A plain CNN deployed with exactly zero inference overhead**, because the auxiliary head is discarded — unlike scattering/Gabor/PCANet front-ends that persist at test time.
4. **An explicit, seed-averaged demonstration of data-scale monotonicity** (positive-or-neutral everywhere), contrasted against a forward-path variant that provably hurts in a mid-data band — a "harmless-at-scale" prior, which the Bayesian framing motivates but few methods deliver by construction.

**The 2–3 papers a reviewer would most likely cite as nearest neighbors:** (a) **Bhattarai et al. 2023** (joint hand-crafted-HOG auxiliary task — closest mechanism, different task/descriptor); (b) **Wei et al. 2022 (MaskFeat)** (hand-crafted HOG as a prediction target — closest target, different regime); (c) **Romero et al. 2015 (FitNets)** or **Mostajabi et al. 2018** (intermediate-feature regression as a training signal — closest loss structure, learned targets). A thorough reviewer would additionally cite **Oyallon et al. 2017** for the small-sample-prior precedent.

## Recommendations
1. **Frame the paper around the loss-vs-architecture distinction, not "moments in CNNs."** The defensible, novel claim is: *a fixed prior injected as a soft auxiliary target scales gracefully with data, whereas the same prior injected as a hard input constraint incurs a mid-data penalty.* Lead with this contrast; it is what the literature does not already contain.
2. **Cite the nearest neighbors head-on and differentiate explicitly.** In related work, dedicate a paragraph each to Bhattarai et al. (2023), MaskFeat (2022), and FitNets/Mostajabi, stating precisely which of the four defining ingredients each shares and which it lacks (use the comparison table). Pre-empting the "isn't this just distillation / just MaskFeat?" reviewer objection is the highest-leverage move.
3. **Add a direct baseline against a learned-teacher auxiliary target.** To prove the *fixed hand-crafted* target is doing the work (not just "any auxiliary regression"), run a FitNets-style variant where the auxiliary target is a trained teacher's features at the same tap point, and a MaskFeat-style HOG-target variant. If moments match or beat these with zero teacher cost, the novelty is compelling.
4. **Strengthen the "washes out" claim toward a formal statement.** Given the Bayesian posterior-consistency framing, consider a simple proposition or controlled experiment showing the effective λ-influence on the learned representation decays with N — turning "empirically neutral at 100%" into "provably/consistently harmless at scale." Benchmark that would change the recommendation: if any data fraction shows a statistically significant *drop* (beyond seed noise) with the auxiliary loss on, the "never hurts" claim must be softened.
5. **Report the ciFAIR caveat for CIFAR-100 low-data points.** Since low-data absolute numbers are sensitive to train/test near-duplicates (ciFAIR raised CIFAR-100 error by 2.73 pts on average), add a ciFAIR re-evaluation (or at least cite Barz & Denzler) so low-fraction gains are not attributed to duplicate memorization.
6. **Generalize beyond CIFAR-100/ResNet-18 before over-claiming.** The staged next step is one additional dataset (e.g., Tiny-ImageNet) and one additional backbone (e.g., a wider ResNet or a small ViT) to show the penalty-band and the auxiliary-fix are not artifacts of a single recipe. Threshold: if the penalty band or the auxiliary benefit disappears under a different backbone, the mechanism claim needs re-scoping to "recipe-dependent."

## Caveats
- **Thin/contested evidence on the exact configuration.** No located paper tests fixed *moment* features as a joint auxiliary classification target, so MomentAux's central comparison rests on adjacent analogues (HOG, learned teachers, segmentation) rather than a direct prior benchmark; the novelty claim is therefore an *absence-of-prior-art* argument, which is inherently harder to prove than a positive one.
- **MaskFeat's negative multi-task result is a cautionary flag.** The MaskFeat authors found combining a hand-crafted (HOG) target with another target *degraded* accuracy in their masked-prediction setting. MomentAux's positive result is in a different regime (joint with cross-entropy, plain CNN), but reviewers may raise this tension; it should be addressed directly.
- **Authorship/citation checks.** arXiv:1502.05689 is authored by Liu, Mallya, Tuzel & Chen (verify before citing; sometimes mis-attributed). FitNets has no DOI (ICLR/arXiv only). Zagoruyko & Komodakis attention transfer appears as both arXiv:1612.03928 and the ICLR 2017 version.
- **Magnitude of gains is modest and dataset-specific.** The headline +2.8 points at 10% data is a single-dataset, single-backbone figure; absolute low-data CIFAR-100 numbers are also sensitive to near-duplicate contamination (ciFAIR). Cross-dataset replication is needed before claiming generality.
- **Some supporting sources are secondary.** A few mechanistic claims (e.g., learnable-Gabor "degeneration," energy-model biology) rest on review articles or single studies rather than large replications; they are used here as motivating context, not as load-bearing evidence for MomentAux's specific claims.
